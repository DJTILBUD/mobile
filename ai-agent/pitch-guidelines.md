# Pitch Writing Guidelines

## Purpose
This file contains the rules and principles the pitch agent must follow when helping a musician write a sales pitch for a job request on DJTilbud. Every pitch generated must follow these guidelines without exception.

---

## Core principle: Proximal language
Research by Parhankangas and Renko (2022) shows that pitches which speak directly to the reader's specific situation convert significantly better than pitches written in general terms. This is called proximal language - language that creates a sense of closeness and direct relevance.

**In practice this means:**
- Every pitch must reference the specific event the customer has described
- The pitch must feel like it was written for this customer, not copied from a previous job
- Never describe the musician in abstract, general terms without connecting it to this specific job
- The customer should feel the musician has actually read their request

---

## Before generating anything: ask first
The agent must NEVER generate a pitch without first asking the musician for input about the job. At minimum, ask:

1. What type of event is this? (wedding, corporate, birthday, etc.)
2. Is there anything specific about this event you want to highlight in the pitch?
3. Is there anything about your experience that is especially relevant to this job?

Keep questions short. Musicians are on their phone, often between gigs. One or two questions at a time maximum.

---

## Required elements in every pitch

### 1. Personal introduction
- Who the musician is as a person, not just as a professional
- Warm and genuine - not a CV line
- Example: not "I am an experienced DJ with 10 years in the industry" but something that feels like a real person writing to a real person

### 2. Direct connection to the event
- Reference the event type, the occasion, or something specific the customer mentioned
- Show that the musician has read the job description and understood what is needed
- This is the proximal language requirement in action

### 3. Why they are the right fit
- Specific experience relevant to this type of event
- Not a general list of skills - a targeted explanation of fit
- If they have done similar events before, mention it

### 4. Social proof where relevant
- Reviews, past bookings, or recognisable clients if applicable
- Cialdini (2001) identifies social proof as a key persuasion principle - people trust what others have validated
- Keep it brief - one or two lines, not a full list

### 5. Warm, personal tone throughout
- Cialdini's liking principle: people are significantly more likely to say yes to someone they feel a personal connection with
- The pitch should feel like the musician is genuinely interested in this specific event, not just available and looking for work
- Avoid formal or corporate language
- Write as if you are speaking directly to the person

---

## What a pitch must NOT be
- A copy of a previous pitch
- A generic description of the musician without reference to the job
- Fewer than 80 words
- A list of technical skills without personality
- Starting with "I" as the very first word - it reads as self-centred
- Phrases like "I am perfect for this job", "hire me", "I can do this" without explanation

---

## Tone reference
The pitch should feel like a message from a professional who is genuinely excited about this specific event and wants the customer to feel confident choosing them. Warm, competent, and specific.

---

## Length
- Minimum: 80 words
- Ideal: 120-180 words
- Maximum: 220 words
- Mobile-readable - short paragraphs, no walls of text

---

## After generating: offer options
Always generate two or three pitch variations for the musician to choose from or combine. Musicians should feel in control of the final output - the agent is a tool, not an autocomplete.
